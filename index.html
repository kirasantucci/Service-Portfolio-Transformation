Service Division Strategy · April 2026

Phase2 Service Portfolio Transformation

We've organized our service divisions by product type, offering, and delivery model — a working construct for making decisions, monitoring performance, and moving faster on the things that meaningfully shift how we operate. Accounts within a grouping share enough operational DNA — buying patterns, delivery cadence, margin profile, scope discipline, and the team posture they require — that we can apply common plays, spot trends earlier, and pilot changes without disrupting the whole book. We're starting with Shared Services, where replicable processes and agent-powered automations yield the most immediate margin impact, then scaling those tools across Project-Based delivery, Enterprise MRR, and other divisions. This gives us a sharper lens on where to invest, where to protect, and where to convert — and positions Phase2 to compete on both financial performance and commercial innovation.

Portfolio Groupings
How each division is defined — click to expand
Enterprise MRR

Our higher-tier MRR accounts and the project work that's grown out of them. Larger, established relationships where the goal is a protected, fluid team posture. This is our cash cow; priority is to not disrupt it while consistently adding value to reduce the risk of client downsizing. Strong candidate for productized value-adds layered on top — strategy and trends automation, roadmap services, and similar offerings that deepen the relationship and lift margin. We're typically running Optimize-style engagements here, but most are team-based FTE, so our work is cut out for us this renewal season converting more of them to outcomes language.

Mastercard

Broken out on its own. Our largest account by revenue, requires significant TLC, and is actively growing through referrals. The flywheel is working, so we're treating it as a portfolio of one to protect the operating model and make sure we're managing it with the focus its trajectory warrants.

Pharma

Separated because the operating model is fundamentally different. High change order volume; we're actively testing tighter scoping and disciplined change management as a margin lever. Hold-the-line on scope is the experiment here. Longer term we'd like to migrate this into Shared Services, but the cadence is more chaotic and less predictable than our MRR accounts, so it stays distinct until the operating rhythm stabilizes.

Shared Services

Smaller retained teams, generally $250K and under. Affiliated sits here too; piloting this approach now with the Angie connection preserved. Projects that spin out either stay in the grouping or move to project-based depending on shape and size. Long-term vision: an “Ultra”-style baseline subscription with productized bursts of additional services as planned additions — AI Visibility add-ons and similar — running distinctly from Optimize, which stays reserved for Enterprise MRR.

Mobile

A distinct grouping because we already operate with a shared team whose skill set is unique to this work. The cross-account model is already in place; treating it as its own portfolio reflects how it actually runs.

Project-Based

Standalone projects, both legacy and new, that are candidates for conversion into Enterprise MRR or Shared Services depending on the size of ongoing investment the client wants to make. This grouping is essentially our conversion pipeline to either the Shared Services/Ultra-like concept or Enterprise MRR/Optimize with Outcomes concept.

Shared Services Transformation to 63% Margin

Agent-powered operating model targeting EOQ3 2026
Current: 50.81% → Target: 63.0%  |  Gap: $421,500
The Opportunity: Shared Services clients require hands-on delivery but operate under constrained budgets. They can't retain dedicated teams, creating a bottleneck—requests pile up, context-switching taxes our team capacity, and throughput suffers when every task requires human conversation and execution. Our solution: embed agents into the workflow to consolidate intake, eliminate pre-dev overhead, and unlock deeper strategic engagement. This transforms Shared Services from a margin drain into a model of efficiency—one we can replicate across Shared Services Wave 2, Project-Based, and beyond. Clients receive the level of service that we hold ourselves to elsewhere in the business.
The Agent Operating Model: The Agent Operating Model orchestrates requests across multiple intake channels, intelligently routes them through specialized agents (intake, triage, docs, development, and strategic growth), and maintains human oversight at critical quality gates. This ensures we gain the efficiency of automation while preserving the judgment and client context that define great delivery.

Project-Based
$3,491,841|51.81%→58.00%|+$216,280
Delivery Plays to Lift Margin
Lead with discovery — prototype as the deliverable.
Stage 1
Scope creep happens when we are not aligned. Lean into prototypes and proof of concepts to create shared understanding before the full scope is committed. This prevents the 'scope expansion after kickoff' pattern that drives margin loss.
Delivery lead review for projects <55% margin.
Stage 1
Monthly review of active projects flagged with degraded margin by delivery leads. Build the review into the delivery cadence, not as an ad hoc exercise.
Price on outcomes, not hours.
Stage 1–2
As AI accelerates delivery, time spent decreases — but value delivered doesn't. If we price on FTEs or hours, we lose margin as our efficiency improves. Shift project pricing to outcomes: what did the client gain, not how many hours did we log. This requires new SOW templates, sales enablement, and team alignment — start with new projects only.
Sell Motion
Identify best-fit buyers for framing engagements.
Stage 1
Know when to sell the full engagement and when to lead with a framing sprint. Some buyers need a validated problem statement — a discovery, prototype, or POC — before they'll commit to a full build. Others are ready to move. Build the qualification discipline to tell the difference: selling a framing engagement to the wrong buyer wastes time; skipping it with the right buyer leaves margin on the table.
Premium positioning on AI-powered builds.
Stage 1
Premium margin pricing on AI-oriented work — full stop. Any project with 'AI-powered' or 'privacy' in the scope commands significant margin premium. Build this into the rate card and don't discount it.
Implement MRR Decision Tree.
Stage 1
Every project close-out should pitch a path to recurring revenue — but the right path matters. Build a decision framework: does this client go to Shared Services (volume, frequency, multi-domain support) or Enterprise MRR (strategic account, AI expansion potential, multi-year roadmap)? The decision tree removes ambiguity for the team and ensures every handoff lands in the right bucket.
